# Programvareutvikling source-to-platform fit report

Date: 2026-10-02. Branch: `maintenance/pre-e-hardening-source-fit`. Baseline: `95489ea0e08909054a4fa82120b28b131873d40c`.

This is an empirical fit analysis of the authored Programvareutvikling content against the live ProgressPal contracts. It registers nothing, authors no curriculum and changes no production programme. It does not validate the curriculum, the Bokmål, the learner experience or production readiness. Raw probe outputs and scripts are in the evidence package (`p2-*.json`, `p2-source-to-catalogue-mapping.csv`).

**Derived analysis, not authority.** This report interprets the protected Programme #2 authority (`00_CONTROL`, OD-P2-32, Package 21) and the live code. Where it disagrees with that authority, the authority stands. The first revision of this report (candidate C2) did disagree: it classified the static instructional-content layer as `PRE_E_DOMAIN_MODEL_REPLAN_REQUIRED` and future attempt/mastery persistence as `PRE_E_L3_SCOPE_DECISION_REQUIRED`, and it treated bundle size as a pre-emptive owner decision. Independent review found those conclusions wrong. Package 21 names the content layer and renderer as the first integration story (gap G06, delivery clauses DC-01…DC-08) and requires persisted attempts, mastery evidence and retrieval to go through their own schema decision (DC-37, DC-41, G14). C3 read that decision requirement as a deferral; R4 below corrects it. This revision supersedes the C2 classification; section 5 states the corrected rows.

**R4 correction of the C3 (R3) revision.** C3 removed C2's over-expansion but then over-corrected: it placed exercise attempts, evaluators, learner evidence, derived mastery, curriculum-spaced retrieval, checkpoint judgement and lesson/step resume in one `DEFERRED_FUTURE_PHASE_NOT_EPIC_E` bucket. Direct adjudication against the Programme #2 authority (Package 21 DC-09 to DC-37 and the M01/M02 slice itself) shows that was wrong. For M01/M02 these are not one future bundle. Reading or opening content is progress, not competence, and a faithful integration needs the whole learner loop: lesson, active exercise, response, submission, evaluation, feedback, hints and support accounting, retry, durable evidence, curriculum-spaced retrieval, checkpoint judgement, derived mastery and progression. The governing principle is that a current ProgressPal capability gap must not silently downgrade a defensible M01/M02 requirement: add the smallest necessary platform capability where required, and no unrelated future infrastructure. Section 5 rows 15 to 17 and 21 are corrected, rows 24 to 29 are added, and section 5a records the learner loop and runtime split. C3's labels for those rows are superseded, not erased.

**R4.1 correction of the R4 revision.** R4.1 is a bounded documentation successor. It corrects four R4 statements against the protected authority and encodes two current owner decisions, and it preserves every other source-fit conclusion. (1) Every submitted answer is persisted as an attempt (`ExerciseAttempt` is the append-only record of each submitted answer, DC-14, INV-08): `not_evaluable` and `evaluator_error` are recorded and non-counting, where R4 said they create no row. (2) A runtime timeout is `evaluator_error` per DC-19, not a learner-incorrect result: R4's "two failure classes" distinction is withdrawn (section 5a). (3) DC-19 is no longer an open owner decision, and an answer-size ceiling is an engineering derivation, not an owner decision. (4) Language and backup rows are updated for the owner decisions of 2026-10-02: the platform is language-capable, Bokmål is preserved and no localisation work is opened; schema 4 is the new forward backup baseline and exported schema-3 backups are not converted. R4 remains historical candidate evidence, and `PRE_E_HARDENING_REPORT.md` section 12 records the correction.

**R4.2 correction of the R4.1 revision.** After one independent exact-payload review of R4.1, a bounded successor corrects two points and nothing else (`PRE_E_HARDENING_REPORT.md` section 13). (1) The durable sequence is two-stage: the immutable attempt is persisted first and carries no outcome, the evaluator then runs against it, and an immutable linked `evaluation_result` commits afterwards (section 5a). R4.1's wording that the attempt is "persisted with the evaluator outcome" put the later outcome inside the submission record and is superseded. (2) K-3 is not given a fallback: nothing in this report chooses a gate retry rule. Every other source-fit conclusion stands.

## 1. Verdict

- **The catalogue level fits.** `Programme → module → mission` maps to `Programme → Unit → Item` without distortion, with deterministic collision-free IDs, and needs no schema, ownership or backup-format change.
- **One bounded, source-justified platform adjustment was implemented and tested in Pre-E:** the additive shared vocabulary values `module` (unit type) and `lesson` (item type), so a module is not labelled "Week" and a lesson is not labelled "Lecture" or "Lab".
- **The instructional content is Epic E scope, not a domain-model failure.** Lesson bodies, exercise prompts and canonical hints are programme-owned content that needs a small content contract and a shared renderer. It does not disturb `Programme → Unit → Item`. A genuine domain-model stop remains available only if real Epic E implementation proves the hierarchy cannot represent the source without distortion. The durable architecture is an asynchronous programme content-provider boundary; statically emitted per-module chunks are an implementation candidate to measure first, not the architecture (rows 14, 21, 28).
- **The learner loop is Epic E scope and is adjudicated item by item, not as one bundle.** Required: exercises and their widgets, the 227 evaluable exercises that need no runtime, feedback, hints and support accounting, durable learner evidence, lesson and step resume, checkpoint judgement, derived mastery and curriculum-spaced retrieval (`REQUIRED_EPIC_E`, rows 15, 16, 24, 26). Required for faithful M02 clearance but gated by an early bounded spike: embedded executable Python evaluation (`REQUIRED_EPIC_E_SPIKE_GATED`, row 25). Deferred: time-based retrieval scheduling, evidence-gated rewards unless Epic E naturally enables them, M03 onward (`DEFERRED_FUTURE_PHASE`, rows 17, 29). The `ProjectArtefact` concept stays in the authority but has no required concrete representation in the authored slice (`DEFERRED_UNTIL_AUTHORED_NEED`, row 27).
- **Schema 3 is not sufficient for this loop.** Persisted evidence needs an append-only store and a lesson-position store, so schema 4 is proposed in `docs/post-release/epic-e/E_SCHEMA_CHANGE_DECISION_RECORD.md`, **PENDING OWNER ACCEPTANCE**. It is not authorized until the owner accepts it. Epic E does not start without that acceptance and an explicit owner instruction. Every submitted answer becomes an attempt, persisted before evaluation, and its later terminal result, including the non-counting `not_evaluable` and `evaluator_error` outcomes, is a separate linked record.
- **Schema 4 sets the new forward backup baseline** (owner decision, 2026-10-02). The live v3 → v4 upgrade stays safe and additive and native v4 export, import, restore and reset are required, but Epic E does not convert exported schema-3 backups or add a 2 → 3 → 4 path, and older exports may be refused clearly as unsupported. This is an explicit owner-authorized compatibility change.
- **No C8 trigger is established.** Real P2 strings and counts sit inside ranges the shared UI already renders for CS50, and nothing has been rendered with P2 active. A generic shared lesson renderer is not by itself a C8 trigger.
- **Bundle size is measured on the real candidate, not decided from estimates.** The accepted budgets leave about 109 bytes of CSS gzip and about 100 kB of total JS gzip headroom (section 6), and the source estimate for content plus evaluator payloads is above that headroom. That is planning evidence only. No budget change is authorized; Epic E builds the provider-backed candidate, runs the real gate, and stops with exact measurements only if the measured candidate breaches a budget.
- **Open owner decisions:** the visible shell brand (OD-6); acceptance or amendment of the schema record; the checkpoint pass rule (K-4) and the gate retry rule (K-3), which are content decisions the app executes and does not invent. DC-19 is not an owner decision (the protected authority already settles it) and neither is the answer-size ceiling (Epic E derives it).

## 5a. Learner loop, runtime split, runtime failure and attempts (R4, amended by R4.1)

**The required loop.** lesson content → active exercise → learner response → submission → deterministic or structured evaluation where supported → feedback → hints and support accounting → retry or remediation → durable learner evidence → curriculum-spaced retrieval → checkpoint judgement → derived mastery and readiness → progression. Reading or opening content is progress. It is not competence.

**Runtime requirement split by exercise type** (`RUNTIME_REQUIREMENT_SPLIT_BY_EXERCISE_TYPE`). Counts re-derived from `pilot.json` (SHA-256 `577cd120b514d3df1445380e516916e1816dac16467311171d28764df7d96bc5`). The C3 proposal to make every runtime-dependent mastery gate non-counting is rejected.

| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Evaluated without a runtime                  | choice, classify, order, match, assemble, blank-match, output-match, table-match, command-regex, diagnose, paste-regex | 227       | no                                                       |
| Executes learner code                        | program                                                                                                                | 42        | yes                                                      |

| No result by design                          | self-check, self-report                                                                                                | 15        | no                                                       |

The 21 `paste-regex` exercises are evaluated but are self-reported: they never create counting evidence for a gate or retrieval (INV-04).

**M01.** Its critical-gate skills (`SK-THK-01`, `SK-CMP-01`, `SK-DBG-01`, `SK-GIT-01`, `SK-AI-01`) have gate items that need no runtime, so M01's authored critical requirements can be met without embedded Python. The one runtime-dependent M01 gate item, `S1-M01-CHK-E01`, evidences `SK-PRG-02`, a required-supporting skill with target Practised. It may stay non-counting until executable evaluation exists, provided no false mastery claim is made and the learner sees an honest state.

**M02.** Faithful clearance requires executable Python evidence. The checkpoint's construction task is `S1-M02-CHK-E01` (learner-written test table, evaluated by executing the trusted reference), `S1-M02-CHK-E02` and `S1-M02-CHK-E03` (programs that must actually run); `E04` is a non-gating pasted item. These carry the critical skills `SK-TST-01`, `SK-TYP-02` and `SK-VAR-01`, whose authored outcomes involve writing or running code. Prediction, trace and assemble items exist for the same skills, but they must not substitute: `SK-TYP-02` and `SK-VAR-01` must not reach `Demonstrated` on recognition-style evidence alone where the authored requirement is code-writing evidence. Which gate items a skill needs is the unauthored K-4 rule (row 26); until it exists the `c` task is a judged criterion under DC-29 and nothing replaces it. Missing required counted evidence blocks mastery. It is never silently removed from the denominator, and the app says "executable evaluation unavailable", never "cleared".

**Runtime failure and timeout (R4.1).** The R4 revision split timeouts into a learner-incorrect class and an infrastructure class. That distinction is withdrawn. Protected DC-19 says an evaluator failure (runtime unavailable, timeout in the app's runtime, internal error) is an app problem, "MUST NOT count as an incorrect answer" and "MUST NOT create evidence either way", and the content contract (`04`, `outcome_rules`; EV-09) defines `evaluator_error` as "timeout in the runtime, runtime unavailable", never counted for or against the learner. So every runtime timeout, including the runtime terminating code that exceeds the authored `timeout_s` (5 on all 42 `program` evaluators), is `evaluator_error` until higher Programme #2 authority explicitly defines a learner-negative timeout. The learner is told the evaluation could not be completed, no positive or negative mastery evidence exists, and a retry is allowed. The spike may diagnose whether the worker and runtime are healthy, for example with a known-good canary program, and that diagnosis does not override the learner-outcome contract. DC-19 is therefore not an open owner decision.

**Every submitted answer is an attempt (R4.1).** The `ExerciseAttempt` definition (`04`; DC-14, INV-08) is the append-only record of each submitted answer, with the first submitted answer immutable and a retry a new attempt. Lifecycle: a capability known to be unavailable before submit shows an unavailable state and creates no attempt; once the learner submits, the immutable attempt commits first, with no outcome; the evaluator then runs against that persisted attempt; an immutable `evaluation_result` linked to exactly one attempt commits afterwards, and no feedback is shown before it does (R4.2: R4.1 had the outcome inside the attempt, so an evaluation interrupted by closing the tab could lose the submission and let a later retry become attempt 1); an attempt with no terminal result after an interruption is unresolved and non-counting and is finished on reload, never replaced; `correct` and `incorrect` results may count under authored rules; `not_evaluable` (DC-20, EV-14), `evaluator_error` and `recorded` results are non-counting; a retry is a new attempt where the applicable retry rule permits it (the gate-item rule, K-3, is unresolved). R4 had said the two non-evidence outcomes create no row, which contradicted this. Persisting them does not manufacture mastery evidence: derivation ignores them by rule, and mastery stays derived, never stored.

**Backup and schema implications (R4.1).** Schema 4 adds two stores and sets the new forward backup baseline: native v4 export, import, restore and reset are required, the live v3 → v4 upgrade stays additive, and Epic E converts no exported schema-3 backup and adds no 2 → 3 → 4 path. Older exported backups may be refused clearly as unsupported. This is an owner-authorized compatibility change recorded in schema record section 7.

**`testtable` is not `program`.** Its evaluator compares the learner's expected values with the output of a trusted authored reference executed on the learner's rows, and does not run arbitrary learner code. Reusing the runtime already required by M02 program exercises is the likely smallest architecture, and duplicate JavaScript ports of reference programs only to avoid that runtime are rejected. This is not made an irreversible requirement before the spike.

**Curriculum-spaced retrieval in the authored slice** (`08` section 6). `S1-M01-RET` retrieves skills from its own module; it is consolidation, not separation, and creates no retained evidence alone. The first real separation for M01 skills is `S1-M02-RET`. `SK-THK-01` (2 authored retrieval items), `SK-AI-01` (1) and `SK-CMP-01` (2) can therefore become `Retained`. `SK-TST-01` has none and cannot in the current content; no M02 skill has a later opportunity authored. The app shows nothing for a skill without an opportunity, not failure (K-5). `Retained` is derived from qualifying unsupported evidence after curriculum separation; elapsed time alone creates nothing.

**PWA constraints that bind the design.** ProgressPal is intended to end as an installable PWA, which is not the same as offline-only. The design must stay compatible with installability, the service worker, local durable state, an account-backed copy later, offline-capable learner work, and separately cacheable curriculum and runtime assets. Today's service worker does not guarantee that future lazily loaded content or runtime assets are available offline, so programme content and runtime loading must let a later change cache selected programme material explicitly without putting a whole future programme in the boot bundle. If a required runtime is not locally available and the network is down, the exercise shows evaluation as unavailable and the learner never receives an incorrect result because of it. New app code, curriculum content, evaluator rules and learner evidence are bound together by explicit identities and hashes so that stale combinations are detected (decision record section 5).

The estimate for content plus evaluator payloads therefore exceeds the JS gzip headroom before any renderer, widget, evaluator code or runtime glue is counted. The budget is not weakened to make a first design pass, and content weight alone is not a domain-model replan.

Whatever the placement, Epic E reports where every runtime and content artefact falls under the gate and what the boot and precache cost is, and a placement that moves weight out of the gate's view is an owner decision, not an Epic E default.

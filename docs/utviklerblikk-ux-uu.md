## Sources checked 2026-10-26

These support the claims; the scenarios and activity wording are original. No source is fetched at runtime. Understanding documents explain WCAG, rather than replacing its normative text.

- U01: [GOV.UK experience mapping](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/creating-an-experience-map): ground journeys and friction in users' experiences; the short task flow here is deliberately simpler than a full experience map.
- U02: [GOV.UK making prototypes](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/making-prototypes) and [moderated usability testing](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-moderated-usability-testing): choose the model for the question, use realistic tasks without directing the route, observe and iterate. Two observations justify investigation, not a population-wide claim.
- U03: [WAI accessibility, usability and inclusion](https://www.w3.org/WAI/fundamentals/accessibility-usability-inclusion/) and [accessibility principles](https://www.w3.org/WAI/fundamentals/accessibility-principles/): overlapping goals, disability-related barriers, different ways of using technology, captions/text alternatives and understandable structure. Situational benefits do not replace equal participation as the reason for accessibility.
- U04: WCAG [keyboard, 2.1.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html), [visible focus, 2.4.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-visible.html), [reflow, 1.4.10](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html). The example concerns ordinary form text, not content that inherently needs two dimensions. Menu focus return is the contextual correction in the example, not a claim that every control must behave identically.
- U05: [forms](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/), [labels](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/labels/), [notifications and errors](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/notifications/), [functional image alternatives](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/functional/), [contrast, 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [label in name, 2.5.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/label-in-name.html) and [status messages, 4.1.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html). Matching the name is a defensible voice-input improvement; the activity still asks for actual checks.
- U06: [WAI evaluation tools](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/tools/selecting/) and [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/): automated checks cover only part of an evaluation. Task checks and participation by people with disabilities add different evidence; none of these alone establishes full accessibility or legal compliance.
- Norwegian legal distinction: [Uu-tilsynet's regulation scope](https://www.uutilsynet.no/regelverk/kva-seier-forskrifta/153), [applicable WCAG requirements](https://www.uutilsynet.no/wcag-standarden/wcag-standarden/86) and [status of newer WCAG versions](https://www.uutilsynet.no/fremtidig-regelverk/status-nyere-versjoner-av-wcag/1868). The official listing states 35 requirements for private sector and 48 for public sector; applicability depends on the solution and statutory scope. Public-sector obligations use EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.1; WCAG 2.2 recommendations are not automatically Norwegian minimum law. The learner card intentionally avoids memorizing counts or advising on a particular entity's obligations.
- [Future Norwegian regulation](https://www.uutilsynet.no/regelverk/fremtidige-regelverk-og-krav/747) distinguishes the EU EAA date from delayed EEA/Norwegian implementation. No EU date is taught as a Norwegian effective date.
